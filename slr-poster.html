Impacts of Sea Level Rise on Scania
A GIS-based assessment of coastal inundation under 2 m, 5 m, and 10 m scenarios · Southern Sweden
Adriana Koleva · May 2026
Abstract
This study uses GIS-based inundation modelling to assess the impacts of sea level rise on Scania, southern Sweden — the country's most exposed coastal region. Three scenarios (+2 m, +5 m, +10 m) are analysed against a 1 m Digital Elevation Model. Results show that a 2 m rise would inundate 197 km² of land, displace over 66,000 people, and put 357 km of roads and 75 km of railroad at risk. At 10 m, nearly 10% of Scania's total area would be submerged, with over 318,000 people displaced and more than 1,000 km of public roads affected. Arable land is the most extensively impacted land-use category across all scenarios. The findings highlight the urgent need for proactive coastal adaptation planning, particularly in the low-lying eastern and northwestern coastal zones.
Introduction

Sea level rise is among the most consequential impacts of global climate change. Scania is especially vulnerable: while northern Sweden is shielded by glacial isostatic uplift, a rise of close to 70 cm at southern Baltic stations is projected by 2100 under RCP8.5 — the highest of any region in Sweden (Hieronymus & Kalén 2020).


This study assesses inundation extents and impacts on infrastructure, land use, and population under three scenarios: +2 m, +5 m, and +10 m above current mean sea level.

Methods

Inundation surfaces were generated by reclassifying the DEM at 2, 5, and 10 m thresholds.


Roads & railroads: Full segment length counted for any segment intersected (a breach affects the whole segment).


Lakes: Whole lake counted where intersected (saltwater intrusion risk).


Built-up area & land use: Only features entirely within the inundated surface included.


Population: Proportional grid-cell overlay used to estimate displaced persons.

Discussion & Conclusions

Even a 2 m rise would have severe consequences: 197 km² inundated, 44 km² of built-up area lost, 357 km of roads affected, and over 66,000 people displaced. The 10 m scenario scales these impacts dramatically — nearly 10% of Scania's land area inundated and over 318,000 residents at risk.


Agricultural land is the most exposed category, with up to 708 km² of arable land lost at 10 m — a major threat to regional food production. The eastern coast (Kristianstad area) and the northwest are the most vulnerable zones.


Limitations: The static bathtub model does not account for storm surges or wave action. Population estimates are proportional approximations. DEM resolution constrains spatial precision.


Recommendations: Prioritise flood defences and managed retreat in the Kristianstad lowlands; restrict new development in 2 m risk zones; commission dynamic flood modelling for highest-risk sub-regions.
